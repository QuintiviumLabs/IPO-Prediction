Model architecture

Three-Arm IPO Model

How one target IPO's observable world — its deal traits, the state of its own IPO market (factors F1–F4), and the geopolitical backdrop — becomes a forecast distribution of outperformance vs its own market's benchmark index at four horizons, with the 1-month outperformance as the target.

How a prediction is made

The five stages every forecast passes through, in order:

  1. Gather only what was knowable

    At pricing date t₀: the deal's static traits, and the F1–F4 factor block computed over same-market history — a recent deal's h-day return enters F1/F2 only if its h-th close predates t₀ — plus 21 days of the daily GPR index. Nothing dated t₀ or later is admitted; features.py enforces the cutoff and a prefix-stability test proves even the expanding z-scores are unchanged when future IPOs are deleted from the dataset.

  2. Encode each source in its own arm

    Banks become a mean of learned 4-d embeddings inside the static arm; the factor block passes through a small MLP; a GRU reads the GPR window (with "level only" and "5 summaries" as ablation alternatives).

  3. Condition on the regime (V2)

    FiLM gates rescale z_static and z_mom using γ, β computed from z_gpr. Both start as the identity, so V2 can only depart from V1 where the data rewards it.

  4. Fuse

    The three representations are concatenated (8 + 8 + 4 = 20-d) and compressed by a small MLP into one 8-d representation of the deal-in-its-moment.

  5. Emit a distribution, not a number

    Four horizons (1d / 3d / 1w / 1m, trading days) × three quantiles, non-crossing by construction (the 10th and 90th are the median minus/plus a softplus offset). The short horizons exist only to regularize training; the 1-month median — averaged across five independently trained seeds — is the forecast, and its q₁₀–q₉₀ band is the uncertainty estimate.
