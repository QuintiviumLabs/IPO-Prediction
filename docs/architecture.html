Model architecture

Three-Arm IPO Model

How one target IPO's observable world — its deal traits and syndicate, the state of its own IPO market (recent deals, supply, macro, sector peers), and the geopolitical backdrop — becomes the probability that the deal outperforms its own market's benchmark index at four horizons, with the 1-month call as the target — anchored at the offer price (includes the first-day pop) or at the first close (aftermarket drift only). A quantile-interval head remains available behind model.head.

How a prediction is made

The five stages every forecast passes through, in order:

  1. Gather only what was knowable

    At pricing date t₀: the deal's static traits and syndicate block, and the factor block (F1–F3, macro state, subgroup peers) computed over same-market history — a recent deal's h-day return enters F1/F2 only if its h-th close predates t₀, and peer returns carry an as-of stamp the pipeline validates — plus 21 days of the daily GPR index. Nothing dated t₀ or later is admitted; features.py enforces the cutoff and a prefix-stability test proves even the expanding z-scores are unchanged when future IPOs are deleted from the dataset. The target anchors at the offer price (includes the pop) or at the first close (pure aftermarket drift) — data.anchor.

  2. Encode each source in its own arm

    Banks become a mean of learned 4-d embeddings inside the static arm; the factor block passes through a small MLP; a GRU reads the GPR window (with "level only" and "5 summaries" as ablation alternatives).

  3. Condition on the regime (V2)

    FiLM gates rescale z_static and z_mom using γ, β computed from z_gpr. Both start as the identity, so V2 can only depart from V1 where the data rewards it.

  4. Fuse

    The three representations are concatenated (8 + 8 + 4 = 20-d) and compressed by a small MLP into one 8-d representation of the deal-in-its-moment.

  5. Emit the call: P(outperform)

    One logit per horizon, trained with class-weighted binary cross-entropy on sign(y) — the positive class is weighted by the training fold's n_neg/n_pos, so a pop-heavy period cannot be gamed by always answering "outperform". Short horizons regularize training; the 1-month probability, seed-ensembled, is the forecast. The magnitude of the continuous target — and its moonshot tail — never enters the loss. The original quantile machinery (non-crossing q₂₀/q₅₀/q₈₀, pinball loss, normal-score label transform, per-tail conformal calibration via calibrate_intervals.py) remains one config switch away: model.head: quantile.

  6. Deploy once, score forever

    fit_final.py trains on all labeled deals and saves a bundle — weights per seed, the fold scaler, the label transform, and the feature schema. A brand-new IPO that hasn't traded yet needs only its row in ipos.csv; predict.py --new-only rebuilds its features as of pricing and emits the quantiles. No retraining, and the bundle refuses data whose schema drifted from training.
